The day

How a day runs.

Six steps from a full head to a finished day. After each rest, the next task starts on its own.

  1. Empty your headA blank page each morning. Type everything.
  2. Keep what mattersOn-device Apple Intelligence turns it into tasks.
  3. Pick the winOne line on what makes today count.
  4. Get in itOne task, a clock counting up, feeds closed.
  5. Rest, then goA breathing ring, then the next task starts itself.
  6. Shut it downScoreboard, one line of reflection. Leftovers roll to tomorrow.

The research

Every mechanic starts with a finding.

We didn't start from a timer and add features. We started from what studies say about attention, interruption and ADHD, then built one mechanic for each.

27 studentsinterviewed · CHI 2026

University of British Columbia, CHI 2026 [1]

Neurodivergent students who use Forest, Freedom and Apple Screen Time said rigid designs clash with how they focus. Fixed 25-minute blocks cut off hard-won hyperfocus, and many switched blockers off to watch something calming as a way to self-regulate.

So we builtA clock that counts up and never ends your session, nudges in a tone you choose, and rests that open your apps on purpose.
251 + 372adults, two studies

Adults with more ADHD symptoms reported hyperfocus more often, across work, hobbies and screen time.

Later studies are mixed on whether diagnosed adults differ.

So we builtA system that protects the deep stretch instead of scheduling around it.

Hupfeld, Abagis & Shah, 2019 [2]

23 min 15 sto resume, on average

Interrupted work that was picked back up the same day took 23 minutes and 15 seconds on average to get back to.

So we builtJuggling with one-tap switching, and Waiting to park a blocked task.

Gloria Mark, UC Irvine [3]

1 short planbefore you switch

A quick "ready-to-resume" note on where to restart reduced attention residue and improved performance on the next task.

So we builtA "where I left off" breadcrumb on every task you switch away from.

Leroy & Glomb, 2018 · Organization Science [4]

Plan ≈ donefor your head

Unfinished goals keep pulling at attention. Making a concrete plan for them reduced that pull, before any of the work was done.

So we builtA morning brain dump that turns everything in your head into a task list.

Masicampo & Baumeister, 2011 · JPSP [5]

d = 0.36vigor, across 22 samples

Micro-breaks raised vigor and reduced fatigue. The benefit to performance grew with break length, so harder work needs a longer break.

So we builtRest sized to the work: 20 seconds after a quick task, up to 3 minutes after a long one.

Albulescu et al., 2022 · PLOS ONE [6]

d = 0.4325 comparisons

People with ADHD discount delayed rewards more steeply. A reward now counts for more than a reward later.

So we builtA reward the moment a task is done: a chime, a haptic, a dot on the chain.

Jackson & MacKillop, 2016 · Biol. Psychiatry: CNNI [7]

55 studieson time perception

People with ADHD showed more variable estimates of how long things take. Evidence in adults is still thin.

So we builtTime you can see everywhere: Lock Screen, menu bar, and the shield ("You're 23 min into…").

Marx et al., 2022 · JAACAP [8]

15 millionUS adults with a diagnosis

About half were diagnosed at 18 or older, and adult diagnoses rose 15% from 2020 to 2023. Most focus tools were designed for a different brain.

So we builtFor three moments: getting in, staying in, and getting back in.

AP, 2025 [9] · HealthDay [10]
